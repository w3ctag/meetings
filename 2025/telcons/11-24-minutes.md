### [process#49: Review Associates program](https://github.com/w3ctag/process/issues/49)

Jeffrey: Any comments on the Associates program?

Martin: I talked to a couple people. If our goal is to do less of the design-review work because we have design-principles, and we're really just looking for smart people to do some reviewing, then the Associate program being hand-picked people isn't serving us well, since it's a volume problem. What you really need is 30-40 people. Cost to any one person is pretty low. Get 1-2 reviews/year and can dedicate real time. And no daily participation expectation. If the point of the program is then to identify people who consistent deliver above performance, then naturally those people would become visible through that process. But the Associates program doesn't give an opportunity to people outside the hand-picked set.

Jeffrey: To do that we might generate something like a CG with something like the private brainstorming repo, but not private.  Then the TAG doesn't design review.  We might have a TAG person (or two) chairing that CG to run it.

Marcos: I think that model misses the point of the reviews we're doing.  I like the idea of a larger pool of experts.  But still I would worry that the group wouldn't have the level of oversight that we have.  Seeing the overall design of the platform.  Maybe it would.  But that's our responsibility.  Other horizontal review groups don't do that

Martin: Couple of things counter that: 1) With TAG people involved in the day-to-day, that group could escalate. "This is a challenging review, and we want a second set of eyes." "There are interesting new principles here." Having the TAG available but not responsible directly is fine. 2) Most reviews, aside from the difficult ones, are straightforward. With PING, there's a group of people who are expert at privacy reviews, but you're rolling the dice every time you allocate someone.

Jeffrey: I'm worried about finding enough people.

Martin: There are a number of people who attend meetings and have the possibility of that broad perspective. The pool of attendees is relatively narrow: there are lots of Googlers. 

Jeffrey: Another goal of the program is to let people try out for the TAG. And we don't see associated participate in findings.

Martin: Not a major problem. If the TAG is responsible for design reviews, we should do the reivews, and perhaps the associates are just us cheating. But review groups I've seen, the people who do good reviews get noticed. Can make more of your time available for reviews and do better reviews, and that's a significant effect. I've done a couple IETF Directorates, and it was quite helpful.

Martin: Other work is what elected and appointed members are for.

Jeffrey: The other problem is that the TAG hasn't been willing to drop work. Would we be willing to delegate?

Martin: Might be "in addition to." If review body is willing to do the work, TAG can be more selective. Right now, there's no safety net and no fallback.

Then there was the minting of identifiers, DC identifiers convert to an enum.  There are restrictions in browsers, such as not including a dot (".").  ISO had "org.iso.whatever", which didn't work with the WebKit IDL parser, whcih converts the values into code.
